How the ranking works, and what changed since 23 September

The rule below is this sheet's own, written for a package that books ten meetings.

  • 1. Venture documented. An allocator scoring 4 or 5 on the 23 September rubric, and the score orders the tier.
  • 2. No route in we can use. No named person whose seat can take a fund commitment, on our files or confirmed at high confidence. A firm we can reach ourselves goes to Tier B instead.
  • 3. Institutional weight. Sovereign backed, public pension, fund of funds platform, endowment and insurer ahead of private wealth.
  • 4. AUM. Size last, and only inside a weight band.
  • Why leg 2 exists. A facilitated meeting is the only door into some firms and a wasted slot at others. Spending one where Red Tree already holds a named person buys an introduction it could have made itself.
  • What counts as a route. A named person whose seat can take a fund commitment, held on Red Tree's own files, or confirmed at high confidence at a firm small enough that the named person is the decision. A listing on the Master LP Database with no name attached is not a route, which is why Pantheon and Quadrille Capital stay in Tier A.
  • The seat read. A title that cannot commit does not earn a slot. That demotes Neuberger and Merrill Lynch, and it makes the CF Private Equity ask conditional on its fund of funds team joining.
  • Score 5. Allocator, venture fund commitments documented in public sources, and we hold a route in: a warm path, a named contact on one of the master lists, or a named public individual at the firm.
  • Score 4. Allocator with venture fund commitments documented but no route in yet, or an allocator with venture named in the mandate and no public commitment to point at.
  • Score 3. Allocator with a private markets programme where venture is plausible but nothing public documents it.
  • Score 2. Allocator whose documented mandate sits outside venture, or a firm we could not identify well enough to rule in or out.
  • Score 1. Not an allocator: GP, bank, broker dealer, placement agent, law firm, exchange or fund services firm. Kept in the sheet, parked at the bottom.
  • The itinerary de-select. Teacher Retirement System of Texas and Texas Municipal Retirement System both come out of the Tuesday and Wednesday itinerary and go into the Carmo asks instead, so the facilitated slot is now the way to see them. Texas Treasury Safekeeping Trust Company stays on the itinerary, because the only Carmo seat near it is a former employee attending unaffiliated.
  • Nothing dropped. The 6 October list carries all 80 firms from 23 September plus 18 new names, one of which is Red Tree.
  • Two names arrived on the 23 September rows. Carmo's own 2025 Texas agenda names Benjamin Bayles as Principal at Teacher Retirement System of Texas, and Peter Teneriello is confirmed as Director at Pomifer Capital.
  • Three Carmo titles merge two seats. CF Private Equity, Cypress Creek Partners and 1900 Wealth each list a concatenated title, so one name will never match the string cleanly.
  • Two prior names did not survive a check. The Neuberger seat has no public Client Advisor at Managing Director rank, and Stafford Capital Partners has two Austin Vice Presidents with nothing to separate them. Both stay at not found.
  • What the reconciliation changed. Merrill Lynch moved from 2 to 3 on Bank of America's September 2025 platform release. Hazoor Partners is typed as a multi family office rather than an RIA. The Affinity Asset Advisors name came off, because neither pass sourced it. Wafra keeps $30 bn, because Wafra's own July 2026 release is dated and the competing figure of about $29 bn is not.
  • Counting note. 12 allocators have a Texas head office. 19 allocators carry some Texas relevance, which is the wider figure the 23 September sheet used.
  • No contact details here. Names, titles and email addresses sit on the Warm paths sheet of the workbook only.
